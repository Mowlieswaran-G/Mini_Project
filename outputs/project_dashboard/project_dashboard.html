Model Architectural Differences & Peak Extrapolation
Tree-based ensembles (Random Forest) partition training data into discrete leaves and cannot predict values higher than the maximum target observation in the training partition (~7,746 MW). During the severe heatwave peak of June 2024 (8,631.53 MW), Random Forest under-predicted by -954.21 MW (-11.05%). Linear Regression maintained unbounded linear slope extrapolation (-71.12 MW / -0.82% error), while PyTorch LSTM's continuous activation layers cut the tree-based under-prediction error by more than half (-451.86 MW / -5.23% error).

4. Documented Project Assumptions & Limitations
Essential technical context for grid operators, evaluators, and system reviewers
Key Engineering & Modeling Limitations
  • Historical Telemetry Boundary: Models are trained on historical 2021–2024 dispatch telemetry. There is no live SCADA or IoT connection to Delhi SLDC; real-time dispatches are simulated via manual or recent-interval inputs.
  • Missing Interval Handling: The Delhi grid telemetry records 1,150 irregular gap transitions (>5 minutes) and 21,569 missing 5-minute slots across 4 years. The project avoided synthesizing fake rows; missing autoregressive slots were handled causally.
  • Recursive Forecast Error Accumulation: In multi-step recursive forecasting (24-hour and 7-day horizons), lag errors accumulate across forward steps. Forecast confidence intervals widen significantly over multi-day horizons.
  • Upstream Weather Dependence: Demand projections are conditioned upon atmospheric weather (temperature, humidity, dew point, pressure). The accuracy of forward projections is bounded by the quality of upstream numerical weather prediction (NWP) forecasts.
  • Extreme Value Bias: All models show a slight negative mean percentage error (-0.56% to -0.90%) on peak days, indicating conservative tracking during sudden unprecedented heatwave ramps.
